The increasing reliance on AI agents in critical systems has exposed vulnerabilities that demand immediate attention. Two recent incidents underscore the risks of overconnected AI systems and the importance of controlled environments for testing and deployment. These events serve as a reminder for developers to prioritize governance and security in their AI frameworks. 

## The Case for Offline Testing

Anthropic’s decision to disconnect its internal evaluations from the internet [[1]](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1009286/anthropic-is-cutting-off-its-internal-evaluations-from-the-internet) is a significant move toward mitigating unintended model actions. By isolating its agents during testing, Anthropic aims to prevent unpredictable behaviors that could arise from external interactions. This approach highlights the necessity of controlled environments for evaluating AI systems, especially as they grow more complex. Developers building agents must consider similar strategies to ensure their models behave as intended before deployment.

## Physical Vulnerabilities of AI Infrastructure

The attack on a Russian AI data center [[2]](https://arstechnica.com/gadgets/2026/10/ukraines-drones-knock-out-ai-data-center-belonging-to-russias-google/) illustrates another layer of risk: the physical vulnerability of AI infrastructure. Supercomputers used for training AI models were damaged, disrupting critical operations. This incident serves as a stark reminder that AI systems are not just vulnerable to cyber threats but also to physical attacks. Developers must account for these risks when designing and deploying AI systems, ensuring redundancy and security measures are in place.

## Balancing Connectivity and Control

These events collectively emphasize the need to balance connectivity with control. While internet access enables AI agents to leverage vast resources and real-time data, it also introduces risks—both digital and physical. Developers must design systems that can operate effectively while minimizing exposure to external threats. This includes implementing robust governance frameworks, conducting thorough evaluations in isolated environments, and securing physical infrastructure.

For those building AI agents, the practical takeaway is clear: prioritize governance, security, and controlled testing environments. By doing so, developers can mitigate risks and ensure their agents operate reliably and safely in the real world.
