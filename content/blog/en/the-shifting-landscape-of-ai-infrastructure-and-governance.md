The landscape of AI infrastructure and governance is undergoing significant shifts, driven by both technological and regulatory forces. These changes are not just abstract concerns—they directly impact how developers build and deploy AI agents. Two recent developments underscore this evolution: the abandonment of a high-profile energy project for AI data centers and a court ruling that reinforces the constraints on AI model capabilities in sensitive applications. Together, they paint a picture of a field that is becoming both more complex and more constrained. 

## Energy Constraints and AI Infrastructure

The decision by Crusoe to abandon its $1.25 billion plan to use Boom turbines for AI data centers [[1]](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/25/crusoe-abandons-1-25b-plan-to-use-boom-turbines-at-ai-data-centers/) is a stark reminder of the challenges in scaling AI infrastructure sustainably. Energy consumption has long been a critical bottleneck for AI development, particularly as models grow in size and complexity. The initial promise of Boom's stationary power plants was to provide a more efficient and scalable energy solution for data centers. However, the project's cancellation suggests that even innovative energy solutions face significant hurdles in implementation. For developers, this means that energy efficiency will remain a top priority when designing AI agents. The focus may shift toward optimizing existing infrastructure or exploring alternative energy sources, rather than relying on unproven technologies.

## Governance and Model Constraints

In another significant development, a court ruling has upheld the ability of entities like Trump to blacklist AI providers such as Anthropic for refusing to enable certain features in their models [[2]](https://arstechnica.com/tech-policy/2026/09/court-rules-trump-can-blacklist-anthropic-for-refusing-to-enable-claude-features/). The ruling emphasizes the potential risks of "overly constrained AI models" in critical applications, particularly in military operations. This decision highlights the growing tension between AI developers and regulatory bodies over the balance between innovation and control. For developers, it underscores the need to design agents that can operate within increasingly stringent governance frameworks. This may involve building more adaptable models that can meet diverse regulatory requirements without compromising functionality.

## Practical Implications for Developers

These developments have immediate practical implications for developers building AI agents. First, energy efficiency must be a core consideration in agent design, as the infrastructure supporting AI continues to face challenges. Second, developers must anticipate and navigate a complex regulatory landscape, ensuring that their agents comply with evolving governance standards. This may require more modular and flexible architectures that can be easily adjusted to meet specific constraints.

In conclusion, the recent shifts in AI infrastructure and governance highlight the need for developers to adopt a more holistic approach to agent design. By prioritizing energy efficiency and regulatory compliance, developers can build agents that are not only powerful but also sustainable and adaptable in an increasingly constrained environment.
