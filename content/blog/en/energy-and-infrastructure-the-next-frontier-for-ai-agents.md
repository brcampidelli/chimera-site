The rapid expansion of AI capabilities is no longer just a matter of software innovation—it’s increasingly tied to the availability of energy and infrastructure. As AI models grow in complexity and scale, the resources required to train and deploy them are becoming a critical bottleneck. Recent developments highlight how this shift is influencing the AI ecosystem, particularly for those building AI agents. 

## Energy as a Strategic Resource

Google’s recent agreement with Constellation to expand nuclear energy capacity underscores the importance of securing reliable and scalable energy sources for AI development [[1]](https://www.infomoney.com.br/business/google-fecha-acordo-com-constellation-para-ampliar-oferta-de-energia-nuclear/). The deal, which aims to add 890 megawatts of capacity, reflects a broader trend among tech giants to ensure their AI ambitions aren’t constrained by energy limitations. For developers of AI agents, this signals a future where access to energy could become as crucial as access to computational power. Building agents that are energy-efficient or capable of leveraging distributed energy resources may soon be a competitive advantage.

## Infrastructure Expansion in Emerging Markets

Meanwhile, companies like EVEO are focusing on expanding AI infrastructure in regions like Brazil, aiming to capitalize on growing demand [[2]](https://iabrasilnoticias.com.br/eveo-quer-ampliar-clusters-de-ia-no-brasil-e-mira-receita-de-r-500-milhoes-ate-2030/). Their plans to scale clusters dedicated to AI suggest a shift toward localized, specialized infrastructure. For agent developers, this could mean greater flexibility in deployment options, particularly in markets where cloud providers may not yet have a strong presence. It also highlights the importance of designing agents that can operate efficiently in diverse infrastructure environments.

## Funding and the Future of AI Startups

The rise of startups like Deepseek, backed by major players such as CATL and Tencent, further illustrates the growing intersection of AI, energy, and infrastructure [[3]](https://the-decoder.com/catl-and-tencent-back-deepseeks-ballooning-funding-round-as-the-ai-startup-eyes-a-2027-ipo/). With billions in funding, these companies are positioning themselves to address the resource challenges facing AI development. For agent builders, this could lead to new opportunities for collaboration or integration with emerging platforms that offer optimized energy and infrastructure solutions.

## Practical Implications for Agent Developers

For those building AI agents, these trends point to a future where energy and infrastructure considerations will play a central role in design and deployment. Developers may need to prioritize energy efficiency, adaptability to diverse infrastructure setups, and compatibility with emerging energy solutions. As the AI landscape evolves, the ability to navigate these resource constraints could become a defining factor in the success of AI agents.
