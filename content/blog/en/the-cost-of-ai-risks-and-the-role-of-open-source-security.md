The valuation of AI companies is no longer just about revenue or market share—it’s increasingly tied to the existential risks their technologies might pose. Anthropic’s potential $2 trillion valuation [[1]](https://www.infomoney.com.br/mercados/ipo-da-anthropic-gera-dilema-como-precificar-os-riscos-de-uma-ia-fora-de-controle/) highlights a growing dilemma for investors: how to price the risks of AI systems that could spiral out of control. This uncertainty isn’t just a financial problem; it’s a technical one. As AI systems become more complex, the need for robust security measures grows exponentially. For developers building AI agents, this means prioritizing security isn’t optional—it’s foundational. 

## The Security Gap in Open-Source AI

Open-source AI projects are particularly vulnerable to security risks. Unlike proprietary systems, where vulnerabilities might be hidden behind corporate walls, open-source code is exposed to scrutiny—and exploitation. Anthropic’s launch of OSS Scanner [[2]](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1008521/anthropic-open-source-oss-scanner), a free service that generates AI-powered vulnerability reports for open-source projects, is a step in the right direction. However, it also underscores a broader issue: many open-source developers lack the tools or expertise to identify and mitigate security risks effectively. This gap isn’t just a technical challenge; it’s a trust issue. If developers can’t ensure their AI agents are secure, users won’t trust them—and neither will investors.

## Lessons from the Theranos Scandal
The Theranos scandal serves as a cautionary tale for the AI industry. The detailed simulation of Elizabeth Holmes’ desk [[3]](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/08/pretend-youre-sitting-at-elizabeth-holmes-desk-on-this-weirdly-detailed-website/), complete with thousands of emails and documents, reveals how easily trust can be eroded when transparency and accountability are absent. While AI developers aren’t facing fraud allegations, the parallels are clear: without rigorous security and transparency, even the most promising AI projects risk losing credibility. For developers, this means adopting tools like OSS Scanner isn’t just about fixing bugs—it’s about building trust with users and stakeholders.

## What Developers Can Do

For developers building AI agents, the path forward is clear. First, integrate security scans into your development pipeline. Tools like OSS Scanner [[2]](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1008521/anthropic-open-source-oss-scanner) can help identify vulnerabilities early, reducing the risk of exploitation. Second, prioritize transparency. Document your code thoroughly, and be upfront about its limitations. Finally, stay informed about the broader risks associated with AI. As Anthropic’s valuation debate [[1]](https://www.infomoney.com.br/mercados/ipo-da-anthropic-gera-dilema-como-precificar-os-riscos-de-uma-ia-fora-de-controle/) shows, the stakes are higher than ever. By addressing security and trust head-on, developers can build AI agents that are not only powerful but also reliable.
